Task Detail redesign — option review

Task Detail (monitoring): three directions

Same goal for all three: a first-time viewer should know, within one second, whether this CDC task is caught up with the source or falling behind, then be able to drill into throughput, lag history and data verification without leaving the page. Each option uses real field names and values from the app, including position/LSN, the nine monitor.log counters and the Data Verification table; nothing is invented. Open one, then use the toggle in its top bar to see how it communicates a healthy vs. a lagging task.

A Verdict First

One dominant hero answers the question; everything else is secondary.

A large status hero — icon, "In sync" / "Falling behind", lag and last-event-age — sits above everything else on the page, with a small inline trend line. Facts, metrics and verification are condensed below it. Safest option: minimal new interaction, biggest jump in glanceability over the current layout.

B Status Bar + Dashboard

The verdict stays visible while you scroll into the details.

A slim sticky status strip (verdict, lag, last event, actions) sits just under the breadcrumb and stays on screen. Below it, lag history, metrics, task facts and verification are all laid out at once in a two-column dashboard — nothing is collapsed. Best fit if you regularly go straight from "is it behind?" to "how far, since when, on which table?" in one motion.

C Live Pipeline

The sync verdict is read directly off the moving pipeline, not a separate widget.

Source, buffer and target are drawn as a live flow diagram with throughput/latency annotated on the connecting segments; the verdict is the diagram's caption. When lag grows, the flow visibly stalls at the buffer instead of just changing a number. Most distinctive option — leans on the fact this product moves data through a pipe, at the cost of being the newest pattern in this app.
